## Statement of the rule (paraphrased)

Rule 21.15 requires that the pointer arguments to `memcpy`,
`memmove`, and `memcmp` point to qualified or unqualified versions
of *compatible* types.

## Where it is deviated

Four call sites, all in `runtime/src/float.c`:

- `float.c:45` — `memcpy(&f, &v, sizeof(f))` where `f` is `float` and
  `v` is `uint32_t`
- `float.c:52` — `memcpy(&bits, &v, sizeof(bits))` where `bits` is
  `uint32_t` and `v` is `float`
- `float.c:59` — `memcpy(&d, &v, ...)` where `d` is `double` and `v`
  is `uint64_t`
- `float.c:66` — `memcpy(&bits, &v, ...)` where `bits` is `uint64_t`
  and `v` is `double`

The `float`/`uint32_t` and `double`/`uint64_t` pairs are not
compatible types under MISRA's definition; they are pairs of
representations that share a bit pattern.

## Justification

Bit-pattern reinterpretation between `float` and `uint32_t` (and
between `double` and `uint64_t`) is required by REQ-FS-004: all
floating-point logic in the runtime must be on the integer bit
pattern, with `float` / `double` appearing only at the boundary
representation. Without the `memcpy` reinterpret, we would need
either:

- Type punning through a `union`, which is a MISRA Rule 19.2
  deviation **and** is undefined behaviour under the strict
  aliasing rules of some compilers, or
- A `reinterpret_cast` (C++) or `(uint32_t *)&v` (C) cast, which
  violates MISRA Rule 11.x and the strict aliasing rules.

`memcpy` between same-sized objects is the **portable, well-defined,
strict-aliasing-safe** way to reinterpret bits in C99. This is also
the pattern used by upstream `FloatUtil.cpp` in
`compiler/extensions/cpp/runtime/src/zserio/FloatUtil.cpp` at
`v2.18.1`, lines 156-198, and by the C standard library's recommended
practice for IEEE 754 manipulation.

## Mitigation

- The four call sites are confined to `runtime/src/float.c` and
  isolated in single-purpose conversion functions
  (`zserio_float_from_u32`, `zserio_float_to_u32`,
  `zserio_double_from_u64`, `zserio_double_to_u64`).
- Each pair is symmetric and individually round-trip-tested in
  `tests/runtime/test_float.c` over zero, negative zero, normals,
  subnormals, infinity, and NaN — see `test_float32_helpers_symmetric`
  and `test_float64_helpers_symmetric`.
- The host's IEEE 754 conformance is implicitly relied upon; this is
  the same assumption every C99 program on every supported automotive
  compiler makes.
